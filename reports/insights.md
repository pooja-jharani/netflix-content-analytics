# Netflix Content Analytics - Findings

Generated by `run_analysis.py` from 7,786 titles. Every number below is computed from the data.

## Headline findings

1. **Netflix's catalogue became international fast.** The non-US share of titles added rose from
   28% (2013-2015) to 63% (2017-2019), a statistically
   significant shift (chi-square p < 0.001). Top countries: United States (2,883), India (956), United Kingdom (577).
2. **Additions peaked in 2019** (2,152 titles) and Q4 is the busiest season:
   about 174 titles a month in Oct-Dec versus 112 in February.
3. **New arrivals are less often brand-new.** 82% of titles added in 2015
   were released within a year; since 2018 that share is 54%.
4. **Kids' content shrank, then recovered.** Its share of additions fell from 27% in 2015
   to 8% (low point), and was 13% in 2020. Adult-rated content
   (TV-MA, R, NC-17) is the largest group at 45% of 2020 additions.
5. **Countries specialise.** Strongest genre over-index (genres with 30+ titles): South Korea: **Romantic TV Shows** (38% of its titles vs 3.9% overall, 9.7x);
   India: **Music & Musicals** (10% of its titles vs 4.1% overall, 2.4x); Japan: **Anime Features** (23% of its titles vs 0.8% overall, 29.1x); United Kingdom: **Docuseries** (15% of its titles vs 4.3% overall, 3.4x).
6. **Movies are getting shorter.** Median runtime fell from 106 minutes
   (release year 2008) to 94 (2020). The Spearman correlation
   between release year and runtime is -0.18 (p < 0.001, n = 4,962): significant but weak.
7. **Most TV shows have one season, in every era.** 63% to
   72% of shows have a single season across all release periods, so this is not
   only a recency effect.
8. **Talent is highly fragmented.** 4,478 directors appear; 81%
   have only one title and the top 10 account for just 2.3% of credits.

## Data quality
- 1 exact duplicate row removed (7,787 -> 7,786 titles).
- Missing: country 507 (6.5%),
  director 2,389 (30.7%),
  cast 718, date added 10, rating 7.
  Missing values are labelled "Unknown", never filled with the most common value.
- 12 titles have a date added earlier than their release year.

## Limitations
- The data is a snapshot that ends 16 January 2021; the last year is incomplete and is excluded from trends.
- It lists titles available at the time, so older titles that were removed are missing (survivorship bias).
- There is no viewership data: "top" means "most titles", not "most watched". Findings describe
  what was in the catalogue, not what viewers chose.
- Only the first listed country is used, so co-productions count once. "International" tags describe
  geography, not genre, and are excluded from genre comparisons.

## Possible business questions this supports
- Which countries and genres are under-represented relative to their share elsewhere (candidates for regional investment)?
- Is the catalogue balanced across age groups, given the drop in kids' content after 2015?
- When should releases be scheduled, given the Q4 concentration of additions?
